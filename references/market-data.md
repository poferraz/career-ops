# Market Data Reference

Single source of truth for hiring-funnel benchmarks, application volume comparisons, sector search durations, remote-work market dynamics, and global compensation sources.

**Evidence note.** Figures and study findings without a primary-source link are unverified historical research notes. The tier labels record the original classification. Check the publication, date, and sample before using a figure in advice. For time-sensitive macro figures, verify current-quarter primary sources before quoting them to a user (`[verify current quarter via primary source]`). If a source cannot be verified, omit the number.

## Required Inputs

Before using market benchmarks, collect:

- Target industry, role, and seniority level
- Geographic region and currency (avoid assuming US/USD norms)
- Work-arrangement preference (on-site, hybrid, remote, or relocation)

## The Hiring Funnel (Reconciled Benchmarks)

Funnel conversion rates vary sharply depending on **channel** (cold job board vs. referral/all-channel) and **stage definition** (initial recruiter screen vs. final-round interview-to-offer). Never mix these definitions in the same claim.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Application-to-initial-screen | ~2-3% | ~5-20% (up to ~50% with strong internal advocate) | Healthcare/retail/education often higher than crowded tech boards | T2 |

### Application Volume Comparisons (Diminishing Returns)

Historical group comparisons across application volume buckets [T2]:

| Volume Bucket | Observed Offer Share | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-10 applications | 27.2% | T2 |
| 11-20 applications | 29.5% | T2 |
| 21-80 applications | 30.9% | T2 |
| 81+ applications | 20% | T2 |

These group comparisons are observational, not causal: candidates who send 81+ applications often face tougher markets or untargeted materials rather than losing odds simply by applying again. Use the user's own conversion rate (`references/job-search.md`) to decide when to recalibrate.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Remote-Work Competition Caveat

**Always include this caveat when a user targets fully remote roles:** supply and demand are heavily imbalanced. Fully remote roles make up a small fraction of total postings (~11-13% historically), are skewed strongly toward experienced talent (~67% experienced vs. ~6% entry-level), and attract far more applicants per opening than hybrid or local roles [T2]. In addition, many employers apply location-based pay bands (40-50% in employer surveys) [T2].

**Practical takeaway:** If a user insists on fully remote work, they should lean heavily on referrals, niche domain positioning, and direct outreach rather than competing cold on national job boards — and compare nearby hybrid roles if speed to offer is their top priority.

- Macro telework participation in major economies has remained structurally above pre-2020 norms (`[verify current quarter via primary source]`, e.g., US BLS CPS telework series or Eurostat Labour Force Survey).

## Job Search Burnout and Mental Health Reality

Historical job-seeker surveys document high rates of search anxiety, burnout, and discouraged workers (`[verify current quarter via primary source]` for macro discouraged-worker counts in BLS/national labor reports) [T1/T2]. These group-level notes never diagnose an individual user's state.

If a user says the search feels exhausting or demoralizing, acknowledge it plainly without dumping statistics, and offer a smaller concrete next step or a deliberate pause. Never solicit medical or mental-health history.

## Quality Checklist

Before presenting market data to a user:

- [ ] Benchmarks match the user's target industry, seniority, geography, and currency
- [ ] Funnel conversion rates clearly distinguish cold vs. referral channels and screen vs. final-round stages
- [ ] Time-sensitive macro figures are flagged for current-quarter primary-source verification rather than quoted as stale facts
- [ ] Search duration figures are framed as historical averages, never personal guarantees
- [ ] Remote-work competition caveat is explicitly included if the user targets remote roles
- [ ] Burnout/stress context is shared with empathy, never as a statistic dump
- [ ] Output passes the anti-slop scoring rubric (35/50 minimum; see `references/anti-slop.md`)
