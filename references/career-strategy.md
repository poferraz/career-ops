# Career Strategy Reference

Use this guide to structure career-planning conversations, calibrate seniority and track moves, frame pivots or gaps, and design concrete skill sprints.

**Evidence note.** Figures and study findings without a primary-source link are unverified historical research notes. The tier labels record the original classification. Check the publication, date, and sample before using a figure in advice. If the source cannot be verified, omit the number.

## Required Inputs

Before advising on career strategy, collect:

- Current role, track (IC vs. management), industry, and tenure
- Target direction (specific role, seniority jump, industry pivot, or exploratory)
- Primary drivers (layoff, growth ceiling, misalignment, lifestyle, or curiosity)
- Constraints the user chooses to share (geography, runway, target pay band)
- Current skills vs. perceived gaps

## Coaching Frameworks and Evidence

Historical RCT notes comparing human career coaching against standard job-search support found materially higher placement and earnings outcomes with structured, recurring coaching [T1]. Because an AI conversation is not the same intervention as ongoing human coaching, focus on surfacing clear options and concrete next steps the user genuinely wants to act on.

| Model | Phases | Best Used When | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GROW** | Goal -> Reality -> Options -> Will | User has a clear target and needs an execution plan | T1 |
| **OSCAR** | Outcome -> Situation -> Choices -> Actions -> Review | Complex multi-path trade-offs requiring side-by-side comparison | T1 |
| **CLEAR** | Contract -> Listening -> Exploring -> Action -> Review | Multi-session or reflective alignment work | T1 |

### Single-Session Compression

In a single conversation, compress the multi-session arc cleanly: **intake (`SKILL.md` 5-Phase Intake) -> accomplishment mining -> strategy decision -> one concrete deliverable or 7-day action**. Only propose a follow-up check-in if the user wants to revisit progress.

## Seniority and Track Calibration

Many stalled searches are leveling or track mismatches. Calibrate positioning, resume scope (`references/resume.md`), and interview examples (`references/interview.md`) to the target tier:

| Level / Track | Primary Scope | Ambiguity Handled | Key Promotion / Hiring Signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Entry / Junior** | Defined tasks and components | Low (clear specs and review) | Learning speed, reliability, coachability, clean execution |
| **Mid-Level IC** | Features, workflows, or accounts end-to-end | Moderate (solves well-framed problems independently) | Autonomous delivery, quality, cross-team collaboration |
| **Senior / Staff IC** | Systems, multi-quarter initiatives, org-wide technical or domain direction | High (frames ambiguous problems and sets standards) | Force multiplication, architectural/strategic trade-offs, business impact |
| **People Manager** | Team hiring, coaching, performance, and roadmap delivery | High (aligns people, priorities, and dependencies) | Team retention/growth, predictable execution, stakeholder management |
| **Director / Executive** | Multi-team org, P&L/budget, multi-year strategy | Very high (external market + org design) | Capital allocation, org scaling, executive influence, measurable business outcomes |

**IC vs. Management pivot check:** Never treat management as the only path to seniority. Clarify whether the user wants hands-on craft leverage (Senior/Staff/Principal IC) or organizational leverage (Manager/Director).

## Cross-Industry Mobility and Pivots

### Open vs. Closed Circuits

Historical mobility notes show sectors vary widely in how easily outsiders enter [T2]:

| Sector | Outside-Field Entry | Circuit Type | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hospitality | ~88% | Open | T2 |
| Retail | ~74% | Open | T2 |
| Logistics | ~69% | Open | T2 |
| Nursing / Licensed Healthcare | ~34% | Closed (credentialed) | T2 |
| Software Engineering | Low without direct proof | Closed (skills-gated) | T2 |

### Financial Reality of Pivots

Cross-industry moves often carry a temporary initial compensation dip compared to within-industry switches [T1]. Flag this honestly so the user can plan runway (6-12 months) and treat the move as a deliberate career-capital investment rather than an unexpected setback.

### Pivoting Into Closed-Circuit Industries

- **Credential gate:** Identify non-negotiable licenses, degrees, or certifications first.
- **Bridge roles:** Target adjacent roles that straddle source and destination domains (e.g., clinical operations -> healthcare analytics; technical support -> solutions engineering).
- **Proof over claims:** Build verifiable public work before asking employers to take a leap of faith.

## Layoff, Career-Gap, and Non-Linear Transition Framing

- **After an involuntary layoff:** Separate job loss from professional competence. Calibrate search duration honestly (`references/market-data.md`), protect weekly energy, and prepare a calm 1-2 sentence explanation (`references/interview.md`).
- **Career gaps:** Keep personal, medical, or family reasons private unless the user opts to share them. Focus documents and interviews on current readiness, relevant past results, and any recent skill refresh the user actually completed.
- **Non-linear transitions:** Connect the dots explicitly for the reader ("Same core problem, new domain"). Use a **Terminology Mapping** table and confirm every translation with the user so it stays accurate:

| What You Did | Source-Industry Phrasing | Target-Industry Phrasing [T3] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinated a large community event | Event coordination | Project management, stakeholder alignment |
| Ran a high-volume restaurant kitchen | Kitchen management | Operations management, shift staffing, P&L control |
| Taught classes and built lesson plans | Classroom teaching | Enablement, curriculum design, training facilitation |
| Managed a retail store | Store management | Revenue ownership, inventory optimization, team coaching |

## The 30-Day Skill Sprint [T3]

A structured way to generate concrete proof-of-work for a pivot or level-up:

1. **Week 1 — Spot a real domain problem:** Study public job postings, industry write-ups, and conversations to pick one recurring workflow pain point your existing skills fit.
2. **Week 2 — Build a small working artifact:** Prototype a dashboard, script, process template, or teardown using tools you know or are actively learning.
3. **Week 3 — Package as a concise case study:** Document the problem, constraints, approach, and measurable or demonstrable result (1-2 pages or a clean repo README).
4. **Week 4 — Share and gather feedback:** Pin it to LinkedIn Featured (`references/linkedin.md`), share it thoughtfully with relevant contacts (`references/outreach.md`), and reference it in applications.

**NDA and IP boundary:** Use **only** public datasets, synthetic/anonymized examples, personal accounts, and original code or templates during a Skill Sprint. Never reuse, upload, or publish a current or former employer's proprietary code, internal data, client information, or confidential playbooks.

## Long-Term Career Frameworks

Use selectively when a user is deciding between multi-year paths:

- **Career Capital (Cal Newport):** Build rare, valuable skills first, then trade them for autonomy, pay, or flexibility.
- **Deliberate Career Progression (Michael Dalton):** Choose each role for the specific capability it unlocks for the step after next.
- **Designing Your Life (Bill Burnett and Dave Evans):** Prototype options cheaply via informational chats, small projects, or shadowing before committing.
- **Lifestyle Design (Tim Ferriss) and Financial Trade-Offs (Ramit Sethi):** Anchor choices in the user's actual weekly life ("ideal Tuesday") and high-leverage financial realities rather than prestige titles alone.

## Accomplishment Mining for Strategy

Run the core accomplishment-mining prompts from `SKILL.md` (Phases 3-4: Accomplishment Mining and Transferable Skill Extraction — decomposition, social proof, quantification probe, and counterfactual) to uncover transferable strengths before choosing a direction. For ongoing career capital tracking, pair those with:

- **Flow signal [T2]:** "Which work tasks make you lose track of time, and which drain you fastest?"
- **RISE weekly habit [T2]:**
  - **Record:** Spend 15 minutes every Friday logging concrete wins.
  - **Include** impact: Note what changed because of your work.
  - **Surface** skills used: Name the specific abilities each win demonstrated.
  - **Embrace** your story: Replace minimizing language with accurate ownership.

## Quality Checklist

Before delivering career strategy advice:

- [ ] Coaching framework fits the decision (GROW for clear goals, OSCAR for multi-path trade-offs)
- [ ] Seniority and track (IC vs. management) are calibrated realistically
- [ ] Cross-industry mobility and potential initial pay trade-offs are stated honestly
- [ ] Layoff, gap, or transition framing protects user privacy and stays factual
- [ ] Any 30-Day Skill Sprint respects strict NDA/IP boundaries (public/original work only)
- [ ] Accomplishment mining from `SKILL.md` was completed before locking in strategy
- [ ] Concrete next steps are defined and pass the anti-slop rubric (35/50 minimum; see `references/anti-slop.md`)
